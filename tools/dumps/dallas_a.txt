==== DALLAS HISTORY & FOUNDING
1. Who founded Dallas in 1841 at a crossing of the Trinity River?
   A) John Neely Bryan
   B) Mirabeau B. Lamar
   C) Sam Houston
   D) Stephen F. Austin
   ANSWER: A) John Neely Bryan
   DID YOU KNOW? Bryan, a Tennessee-born trader, built a cabin on the east bank of the Trinity and is credited as the city's founder.
2. Which U.S. Vice President is Dallas most often said to be named after?
   A) John C. Calhoun
   B) Aaron Burr
   C) George Mifflin Dallas
   D) Hannibal Hamlin
   ANSWER: C) George Mifflin Dallas
   DID YOU KNOW? He served under James K. Polk. The true origin of the name is still debated, and some say it honors a different Dallas entirely.
3. In 1855, European settlers founded La Réunion near Dallas. What was it?
   A) A cattle-drive depot
   B) A frontier army fort
   C) A Spanish mission
   D) A utopian socialist colony
   ANSWER: D) A utopian socialist colony
   DID YOU KNOW? Largely French, Belgian and Swiss, the colonists were inspired by Charles Fourier. The colony collapsed within a few years, but many settlers stayed and shaped Dallas's culture.
4. In what year did a great fire destroy much of Dallas's downtown square?
   A) 1893
   B) 1871
   C) 1860
   D) 1849
   ANSWER: C) 1860
   DID YOU KNOW? The July 1860 blaze probably began when matches ignited in the Texas heat, though panic over rumored abolitionist arson led to harsh reprisals.
5. Which railroad reached Dallas in 1872, followed a year later by the Texas and Pacific, making the city a rail crossroads?
   A) Santa Fe
   B) Houston and Texas Central
   C) Union Pacific
   D) Katy Railroad
   ANSWER: B) Houston and Texas Central
   DID YOU KNOW? The crossing of two rail lines turned Dallas from a small town into a commercial hub.
6. Dallas grew rich in the late 1800s as a major inland market for which crop?
   A) Tobacco
   B) Cotton
   C) Rice
   D) Sugar cane
   ANSWER: B) Cotton
   DID YOU KNOW? Dallas became one of the largest inland cotton markets in the world, and its banks and insurance firms grew alongside it.
7. The State Fair of Texas, held at Fair Park, first opened in which year?
   A) 1886
   B) 1936
   C) 1912
   D) 1856
   ANSWER: A) 1886
   DID YOU KNOW? It is among the largest annual state fairs in the United States.
8. Which newspaper, founded in 1885, is Dallas's major daily?
   A) The Dallas Times Herald
   B) The Texas Tribune
   C) The Dallas Morning News
   D) The Dallas Gazette
   ANSWER: C) The Dallas Morning News
   DID YOU KNOW? It was launched by A.H. Belo and Company, and its publisher George Dealey later gave his name to Dealey Plaza.
9. In what year was the department store Neiman Marcus founded in Dallas?
   A) 1907
   B) 1925
   C) 1887
   D) 1946
   ANSWER: A) 1907
   DID YOU KNOW? It was founded by Herbert Marcus, his sister Carrie Marcus Neiman and her husband Al Neiman.
10. The Adolphus Hotel, opened in 1912, was built by a man famous for what product?
   A) Dr Pepper
   B) Colt revolvers
   C) Stetson hats
   D) Budweiser beer
   ANSWER: D) Budweiser beer
   DID YOU KNOW? Adolphus Busch, the St. Louis brewer, built it, and it is still a downtown landmark.
11. The Dallas neighborhood of Oak Cliff was annexed by Dallas in which year?
   A) 1903
   B) 1948
   C) 1925
   D) 1873
   ANSWER: A) 1903
   DID YOU KNOW? Oak Cliff was once its own city, and today it is home to the Bishop Arts District.
12. Highland Park Village, opened in 1931, is notable as one of the first of what in the United States?
   A) Drive-in theaters
   B) Skyscrapers
   C) Planned shopping centers
   D) Public libraries
   ANSWER: C) Planned shopping centers
   DID YOU KNOW? Its Spanish Revival design became a model for the modern shopping center.
13. The Federal Reserve Bank of Dallas serves which numbered Federal Reserve District?
   A) Twelfth
   B) Fourth
   C) Seventh
   D) Eleventh
   ANSWER: D) Eleventh
   DID YOU KNOW? Dallas was chosen as one of 12 cities for a Reserve Bank in 1914.
14. Dallas Love Field is named for Moss L. Love, who was what?
   A) A Texas Ranger
   B) A Dallas mayor
   C) An Army aviator killed in a 1913 crash
   D) A cotton broker
   ANSWER: C) An Army aviator killed in a 1913 crash
   DID YOU KNOW? Love Field opened as an Army training airfield in 1917.
15. Which wildcatter's East Texas oil strike in 1930 helped make Dallas an oil-finance center, after H.L. Hunt bought his leases?
   A) Howard Hughes
   B) Spindletop's Pattillo Higgins
   C) J. Paul Getty
   D) Dad Joiner
   ANSWER: D) Dad Joiner
   DID YOU KNOW? Hunt made a fortune from the field and based his empire in Dallas.
16. Fair Park hosted what major event in 1936?
   A) The Olympic Trials
   B) The Texas Centennial Exposition
   C) A World's Fair
   D) The Democratic National Convention
   ANSWER: B) The Texas Centennial Exposition
   DID YOU KNOW? Its Art Deco buildings from that event are the largest collection of 1930s exposition architecture in the U.S.
17. Southern Methodist University opened in which year?
   A) 1885
   B) 1915
   C) 1932
   D) 1948
   ANSWER: B) 1915
   DID YOU KNOW? SMU's Dallas Hall was modeled on Thomas Jefferson's Rotunda at the University of Virginia.
18. Dallas Union Station, next to Reunion Tower, opened in which year?
   A) 1889
   B) 1952
   C) 1934
   D) 1916
   ANSWER: D) 1916
   DID YOU KNOW? It was built to consolidate the city's several rail lines into one depot.
19. When it was introduced in 1947, which area code was assigned to Dallas?
   A) 214
   B) 512
   C) 972
   D) 713
   ANSWER: A) 214
   DID YOU KNOW? 214 was one of the original area codes, and 972 and 469 were later added as overlays.
20. The 1892 Dallas County Courthouse, built of red sandstone, is known by what nickname?
   A) The Red Barn
   B) Big Red
   C) The Pink Palace
   D) Old Red
   ANSWER: D) Old Red
   DID YOU KNOW? It now houses the Old Red Museum of Dallas County History and Culture.
21. The Dallas Zoo, opened in 1888, is the oldest zoo in which state?
   A) The United States
   B) Oklahoma
   C) Texas
   D) The entire South
   ANSWER: C) Texas
   DID YOU KNOW? It is often called the oldest and largest zoo in Texas.
22. White Rock Lake was created as a reservoir in what year?
   A) 1911
   B) 1861
   C) 1958
   D) 1936
   ANSWER: A) 1911
   DID YOU KNOW? It is now a popular recreation spot, and local lore holds that a ghostly Lady of the Lake haunts its shores.
23. The song "Big D" popularized Dallas's nickname. In which musical was it introduced?
   A) West Side Story
   B) The Most Happy Fella
   C) Oklahoma!
   D) Annie Get Your Gun
   ANSWER: B) The Most Happy Fella
   DID YOU KNOW? Frank Loesser's 1956 musical gave the city its enduring nickname.
24. El Fenix, founded in Dallas in 1918, claims to be the oldest chain of what?
   A) Steakhouses
   B) Diners
   C) Barbecue joints
   D) Mexican restaurants in the U.S.
   ANSWER: D) Mexican restaurants in the U.S.
   DID YOU KNOW? Founded by Miguel Martinez, El Fenix helped popularize Tex-Mex cooking nationwide.
25. 7-Eleven grew out of the Southland Ice Company, which started in what Dallas neighborhood in 1927?
   A) Oak Cliff
   B) Lakewood
   C) Deep Ellum
   D) Preston Hollow
   ANSWER: A) Oak Cliff
   DID YOU KNOW? An employee began selling milk, bread and eggs from the ice dock, and the store was renamed 7-Eleven for its original 7 a.m. to 11 p.m. hours.
26. Braniff International Airways, based at Love Field, shocked the airline world in 1965 by doing what?
   A) Painting its planes bright colors
   B) Serving full meals for free
   C) Flying only at night
   D) Banning checked luggage
   ANSWER: A) Painting its planes bright colors
   DID YOU KNOW? The "End of the Plain Plane" campaign used designer Alexander Girard and colorful aircraft.
27. Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport began operations in which year?
   A) 1954
   B) 1996
   C) 1984
   D) 1974
   ANSWER: D) 1974
   DID YOU KNOW? DFW covers about 27 square miles, larger than the island of Manhattan.
28. Who was Dallas's first elected woman mayor, serving from 1987?
   A) Kay Bailey Hutchison
   B) Sarah T. Hughes
   C) Annette Strauss
   D) Laura Miller
   ANSWER: C) Annette Strauss
   DID YOU KNOW? Strauss was a popular civic booster whose name is now on a downtown arts square.
29. Who became Dallas's first African American mayor in 1995?
   A) Tom Leppert
   B) Starke Taylor
   C) Ron Kirk
   D) Eddie Bernice Johnson
   ANSWER: C) Ron Kirk
   DID YOU KNOW? Kirk later served as U.S. Trade Representative under President Obama.
30. When Dallas was founded in 1841, Texas was what?
   A) A Spanish colony
   B) A Mexican state
   C) A U.S. territory
   D) An independent republic
   ANSWER: D) An independent republic
   DID YOU KNOW? Texas joined the United States in 1845.
==== NOVEMBER 1963 & DEALEY PLAZA
31. On what date was President John F. Kennedy assassinated in Dallas?
   A) December 7, 1963
   B) October 24, 1963
   C) November 22, 1963
   D) November 25, 1963
   ANSWER: C) November 22, 1963
   DID YOU KNOW? He was shot while riding in a motorcade through Dealey Plaza.
32. Dealey Plaza is named after the publisher of which newspaper?
   A) The Dallas Morning News
   B) The Houston Chronicle
   C) The Dallas Times Herald
   D) The Fort Worth Star-Telegram
   ANSWER: A) The Dallas Morning News
   DID YOU KNOW? George Bannerman Dealey was a longtime civic leader, and the plaza was dedicated in 1940.
33. Which Dallas museum, opened in 1989, is located on the building's sixth floor where shots were fired?
   A) The Perot Museum
   B) The Sixth Floor Museum
   C) The Old Red Museum
   D) The Dallas Heritage Museum
   ANSWER: B) The Sixth Floor Museum
   DID YOU KNOW? It occupies the former Texas School Book Depository.
34. Who filmed the assassination in the famous 26-second home movie?
   A) Clint Hill
   B) Jack Ruby
   C) Abraham Zapruder
   D) Lee Harvey Oswald
   ANSWER: C) Abraham Zapruder
   DID YOU KNOW? Zapruder was a Dallas dressmaker, and the film is among the most studied in history.
35. Where were President Kennedy and later Lee Harvey Oswald both pronounced dead?
   A) Methodist Hospital
   B) Presbyterian Hospital
   C) Parkland Hospital
   D) Baylor Hospital
   ANSWER: C) Parkland Hospital
   DID YOU KNOW? Parkland is a major Dallas public hospital.
36. Who swore in Lyndon B. Johnson aboard Air Force One at Love Field, becoming the first woman to swear in a president?
   A) Judge Sarah T. Hughes
   B) Lady Bird Johnson
   C) Ruth Bader Ginsburg
   D) Barbara Jordan
   ANSWER: A) Judge Sarah T. Hughes
   DID YOU KNOW? The ceremony was held in the stifling heat of the grounded plane.
37. Oswald was arrested in which Oak Cliff movie theater?
   A) The Palace
   B) The Majestic
   C) The Lakewood
   D) The Texas Theatre
   ANSWER: D) The Texas Theatre
   DID YOU KNOW? The theater still stands and now hosts films and events.
38. Where was Oswald shot by Jack Ruby on November 24, 1963?
   A) In the basement of Dallas police headquarters
   B) At the Dallas County jail
   C) Outside Parkland Hospital
   D) At Love Field
   ANSWER: A) In the basement of Dallas police headquarters
   DID YOU KNOW? The shooting was carried live on television.
39. What nightclub did Jack Ruby own?
   A) The Longhorn Room
   B) The Carousel Club
   C) The Cattleman's Club
   D) The Silver Spur
   ANSWER: B) The Carousel Club
   DID YOU KNOW? Ruby ran it on Commerce Street.
40. President Kennedy was on his way to a luncheon at which Dallas venue when he was shot?
   A) The Dallas Trade Mart
   B) The Adolphus Hotel
   C) Fair Park Music Hall
   D) Dallas City Hall
   ANSWER: A) The Dallas Trade Mart
   DID YOU KNOW? The Trade Mart was a wholesale showroom center, and the motorcade was headed there when it passed through Dealey Plaza.
41. The John F. Kennedy Memorial in downtown Dallas, a cenotaph, was designed by which architect?
   A) Renzo Piano
   B) Philip Johnson
   C) Frank Lloyd Wright
   D) I. M. Pei
   ANSWER: B) Philip Johnson
   DID YOU KNOW? A cenotaph is an empty tomb, and Johnson's open-roofed design was dedicated in 1970.
42. What marks the spots on Elm Street where the shots struck?
   A) Two white X's
   B) Small flags
   C) Bronze plaques
   D) Red paint stripes
   ANSWER: A) Two white X's
   DID YOU KNOW? Visitors often dart into traffic to take photos of them, which is dangerous.
43. A month before JFK's visit, which U.S. ambassador to the UN was struck by a protester's sign in Dallas?
   A) Dean Rusk
   B) Adlai Stevenson
   C) Henry Cabot Lodge
   D) Arthur Goldberg
   ANSWER: B) Adlai Stevenson
   DID YOU KNOW? The October 1963 incident was a sign of the tense political climate in the city.
44. Dealey Plaza was designated what in 1993?
   A) A UNESCO site
   B) A state park
   C) A federal courthouse
   D) A National Historic Landmark District
   ANSWER: D) A National Historic Landmark District
   DID YOU KNOW? The plaza and surrounding buildings are protected for their role in the assassination.
45. What does the term "cenotaph" mean, as in the JFK Memorial?
   A) An empty tomb
   B) A reflecting pool
   C) A soldier's grave
   D) A burning torch
   ANSWER: A) An empty tomb
   DID YOU KNOW? The word comes from the Greek for "empty tomb".
==== LANDMARKS, ARCHITECTURE & THE STATE FAIR
46. How tall is Reunion Tower, whose geodesic ball opened in 1978?
   A) 921 feet
   B) 740 feet
   C) 301 feet
   D) 561 feet
   ANSWER: D) 561 feet
   DID YOU KNOW? The ball houses an observation deck and a revolving restaurant.
47. Bank of America Plaza is the tallest building in Dallas at how many feet?
   A) 1,050
   B) 921
   C) 780
   D) 561
   ANSWER: B) 921
   DID YOU KNOW? The skyscraper is outlined in green lights at night.
48. Fountain Place, a glass prism skyscraper downtown, was designed by the firm of which architect?
   A) Frank Gehry
   B) I. M. Pei
   C) Philip Johnson
   D) Mies van der Rohe
   ANSWER: B) I. M. Pei
   DID YOU KNOW? Pei also designed Dallas City Hall and the Meyerson Symphony Center.
49. The red neon Pegasus sign atop the Magnolia Hotel was the logo of which oil company?
   A) Mobil
   B) Shell
   C) Gulf
   D) Texaco
   ANSWER: A) Mobil
   DID YOU KNOW? It began as the symbol of the Magnolia Petroleum Company and has been a Dallas icon since 1934.
50. Dallas City Hall, opened in 1978, features a dramatic what?
   A) Clock tower
   B) Inverted triangular facade
   C) Glass pyramid
   D) Domed roof
   ANSWER: B) Inverted triangular facade
   DID YOU KNOW? I. M. Pei designed the building, which looks like a leaning wall and appears in the movie RoboCop.
51. The Meyerson Symphony Center, opened in 1989, is home to which orchestra?
   A) Dallas Symphony Orchestra
   B) Dallas Opera Orchestra
   C) Texas Philharmonic
   D) Southwest Chamber Orchestra
   ANSWER: A) Dallas Symphony Orchestra
   DID YOU KNOW? It was designed by I. M. Pei and is known for its acoustics.
52. The Dallas Arts District is billed as the largest contiguous what in the United States?
   A) Public garden
   B) Shopping complex
   C) Urban arts district
   D) University campus
   ANSWER: C) Urban arts district
   DID YOU KNOW? It spans 19 blocks and hosts the Dallas Museum of Art, the Nasher, the Winspear Opera House and more.
53. The Nasher Sculpture Center, opened in 2003, was designed by which architect?
   A) Frank Gehry
   B) Renzo Piano
   C) Zaha Hadid
   D) Santiago Calatrava
   ANSWER: B) Renzo Piano
   DID YOU KNOW? Piano also designed the Kimbell Art Museum's addition across the Metroplex in Fort Worth.
54. The Perot Museum of Nature and Science, opened in 2012, was designed by which architect?
   A) Daniel Libeskind
   B) Norman Foster
   C) Frank Gehry
   D) Thom Mayne
   ANSWER: D) Thom Mayne
   DID YOU KNOW? Its cube shape appears to float above the ground.
55. Which Spanish architect designed the Margaret Hunt Hill Bridge over the Trinity River?
   A) Antoni Gaudí
   B) Rafael Moneo
   C) Santiago Calatrava
   D) Ricardo Bofill
   ANSWER: C) Santiago Calatrava
   DID YOU KNOW? The bridge's giant white arch is a signature of the Dallas skyline.
56. Klyde Warren Park, opened in 2012, was built over what?
   A) A parking garage
   B) A railroad yard
   C) A river
   D) A freeway
   ANSWER: D) A freeway
   DID YOU KNOW? It is a deck park over Woodall Rodgers Freeway connecting downtown and Uptown.
57. Thanks-Giving Square's chapel, designed by Philip Johnson, has a spiral stained-glass window called what?
   A) Glory Window
   B) Rose Window
   C) Stars of Texas
   D) Lone Star Window
   ANSWER: A) Glory Window
   DID YOU KNOW? The window by French artist Gabriel Loire spirals upward toward the sun.
58. Pioneer Plaza in downtown Dallas features how many bronze longhorn steers?
   A) 49
   B) 12
   C) 75
   D) 100
   ANSWER: A) 49
   DID YOU KNOW? The steers and three trail hands recall the cattle drives that passed through Texas in the 1800s.
59. General admission to the Dallas Museum of Art has been what since 2013?
   A) Seasonal
   B) Five dollars
   C) Members-only
   D) Free
   ANSWER: D) Free
   DID YOU KNOW? The museum dropped its general admission fee and launched a free membership program.
60. The Kalita Humphreys Theater in Dallas is the only freestanding theater designed by which architect?
   A) Frank Lloyd Wright
   B) Eero Saarinen
   C) Louis Sullivan
   D) Le Corbusier
   ANSWER: A) Frank Lloyd Wright
   DID YOU KNOW? It opened in 1959 and is home to Dallas Theater Center.
61. How tall is the modern Big Tex, the State Fair's cowboy mascot?
   A) 100 feet
   B) 55 feet
   C) 35 feet
   D) 75 feet
   ANSWER: B) 55 feet
   DID YOU KNOW? The original Big Tex was a Santa Claus built in Kerens, Texas, and the State Fair of Texas bought him around 1951.
62. In what year did Big Tex burn in an electrical fire at the State Fair?
   A) 2012
   B) 2018
   C) 2005
   D) 1999
   ANSWER: A) 2012
   DID YOU KNOW? A new Big Tex was unveiled in 2013.
63. What fried treat was introduced at the State Fair of Texas in 1942?
   A) Fried butter
   B) The corny dog
   C) The turkey leg
   D) The funnel cake
   ANSWER: B) The corny dog
   DID YOU KNOW? Fletcher's Corny Dogs are still sold at the fair.
64. The Texas Star at Fair Park is a famed what?
   A) Observation tower
   B) Roller coaster
   C) Giant Ferris wheel
   D) Hot-air balloon
   ANSWER: C) Giant Ferris wheel
   DID YOU KNOW? At 212 feet it was billed as the tallest Ferris wheel in North America.
65. The Dallas Arboretum is on the shore of which lake?
   A) Lake Lewisville
   B) White Rock Lake
   C) Lake Ray Hubbard
   D) Joe Pool Lake
   ANSWER: B) White Rock Lake
   DID YOU KNOW? The garden covers about 66 acres.
66. Deep Ellum gets its name from a pronunciation of which street?
   A) Main Street
   B) Elm Street
   C) Ervay Street
   D) Commerce Street
   ANSWER: B) Elm Street
   DID YOU KNOW? Locals shortened "Deep Elm" to "Deep Ellum".
67. The Bishop Arts District, known for shops and restaurants, is in what Dallas area?
   A) Uptown
   B) Lake Highlands
   C) Oak Cliff
   D) Preston Hollow
   ANSWER: C) Oak Cliff
   DID YOU KNOW? The district is named for Bishop Avenue, and its restored storefronts are now a hub for local shops and restaurants.
68. The Wyly Theatre in the Dallas Arts District is known for being what kind of theater?
   A) A floating theater
   B) A vertical, flexible theater
   C) A rotating theater
   D) An outdoor amphitheater
   ANSWER: B) A vertical, flexible theater
   DID YOU KNOW? Its stage tower can be rearranged in many configurations.
69. Dallas's "Mixmaster" refers to what?
   A) A music festival
   B) A cement factory
   C) A pie-making contest
   D) A freeway interchange downtown
   ANSWER: D) A freeway interchange downtown
   DID YOU KNOW? It sits where several highways come together near downtown.
70. Which wholesale center, opened in the late 1950s, is billed as one of the largest of its kind in the world?
   A) Texas Mart
   B) Preston Hollow Gallery
   C) Dallas Market Center
   D) Fashion District Mall
   ANSWER: C) Dallas Market Center
   DID YOU KNOW? The Dallas Trade Mart was developed by Trammell Crow.
71. The SMU Meadows Museum is known for having one of the largest collections of what outside Spain?
   A) Spanish art
   B) Asian ceramics
   C) Impressionist paintings
   D) Western art
   ANSWER: A) Spanish art
   DID YOU KNOW? It was founded by oilman Algur H. Meadows.
72. In what year was the red neon Pegasus first installed atop the Magnolia Building?
   A) 1974
   B) 1914
   C) 1954
   D) 1934
   ANSWER: D) 1934
   DID YOU KNOW? It was lit up as part of the Magnolia Petroleum Company's advertising.
73. The Majestic Theatre, opened in 1921 in downtown Dallas, originally hosted what kind of shows?
   A) Vaudeville
   B) Opera
   C) Circus
   D) Rodeo
   ANSWER: A) Vaudeville
   DID YOU KNOW? It was part of the Interstate Amusement circuit.
74. The Hall of State at Fair Park, built for the 1936 Centennial, celebrates what?
   A) World religions
   B) Texas history
   C) American presidents
   D) Dallas sports
   ANSWER: B) Texas history
   DID YOU KNOW? Its murals and statues honor figures from the state's past.
75. The Cotton Bowl stadium at Fair Park was built in which year?
   A) 1970
   B) 1950
   C) 1930
   D) 1900
   ANSWER: C) 1930
   DID YOU KNOW? It was originally called Fair Park Stadium.
76. Which sculptor's "Dallas Piece" stands outside Dallas City Hall?
   A) Auguste Rodin
   B) Isamu Noguchi
   C) Alexander Calder
   D) Henry Moore
   ANSWER: D) Henry Moore
   DID YOU KNOW? The abstract bronze was installed in 1978.
77. The Winspear Opera House was designed by the firm of which British architect?
   A) Zaha Hadid
   B) Richard Rogers
   C) Norman Foster
   D) Renzo Piano
   ANSWER: C) Norman Foster
   DID YOU KNOW? It is home to the Dallas Opera and the Texas Ballet Theater.
